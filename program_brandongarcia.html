A note from TRAIN Brandon — welcome to your next 8 weeks with TRAIN.

This block has one big priority: consistency. With work ramping up, the goal isn't to build a program that looks perfect on paper — it's to build one that you can actually execute week after week.

You'll have 5 sessions per week, designed to fit into roughly 45 minutes. The work is intentional: we're giving you enough volume and intensity to continue building strength and muscle without turning every workout into a marathon.

And if life happens and you only have 30 minutes? That's completely fine. Hit the first 3–4 movements, make them count, and get on with your day. Don't try to "make up" missed volume later. One shortened workout will never derail your progress; consistently disappearing because you couldn't do the whole thing will.

You've been training long enough to know that progress isn't about finding the perfect workout — it's about stacking good sessions, recovering well, and giving your body a reason to adapt.

So train hard. Pay attention to how you move. Recover on purpose. Log your work so we can build from it.

Eight weeks. Show up, execute, and let's see what we can build.

— TRAIN

Phase 1 — Establish (Week 1 of 2)
Establish
This program is following a traditional lower/upper split — 5 days a week, four lifting sessions (2 Lower, 2 Upper) plus one Conditioning/Hot Pilates day, built around one priority: staying consistent through 8 weeks while work gets busier. Every session is built to fit inside 45 minutes (60 with Core Work and a full warm-up/cooldown). You'll move through six phases over the block — Establish, Accumulate, Intensify, Push, a Deload, and a Rebuild week — each with its own RIR target, so effort ramps up on purpose instead of just going hard every week. With the hip/low back in mind, a few movements (RDL, Back Extension) have substitutions already built in — flag it any time something feels off and we'll adjust from there.

Weeks 1–2 are about setting a baseline, not chasing numbers. Work all compounds at 2–3 RIR and pay close attention to how the hip/low back responds to each movement, especially Front Squat, RDL, and Back Extension. Whatever you log this week becomes the number you're beating from Week 2 on. Technique and symptom tolerance come first — no PRs yet.
