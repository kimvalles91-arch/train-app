Getting Started
How This Works
01Your Training Program
TRAIN is an intelligent training platform built around progressive overload and real performance data. Your program is structured in 8-week blocks, with each week intentionally building on the one before it. TRAIN tells you exactly what to do each session — including exercises, sets, reps, and progression — while tracking the weight and reps you log along the way.

As you TRAIN, your performance data carries forward week to week, so you always know what you did last time and what you're working toward next. Progressive overload is built into the programming, allowing reps, sets, and load to increase strategically throughout the block.

There's no spreadsheets, screenshots, or guessing what to do next. You simply open TRAIN, log your performance, and come back the following week knowing exactly where to pick up. Week 1 establishes your baseline. Week 8 reflects how far you've progressed.
02Your Nutrition Guide
What you eat plays a major role in how you feel, perform, recover, and ultimately see results. But the goal isn't to put you on a meal plan you have to follow forever — it's to empower you with the knowledge and tools to take charge of your own nutrition.

TRAIN gives you personalized calorie, protein, carbohydrate, fat, and fiber targets, along with meal ideas based on foods you actually enjoy. More importantly, it helps you understand why those nutrients matter, how they fuel your body, and how to make smart choices that support your goals.

Because real life isn't a meal plan. You're going to eat at restaurants, travel, have busy days, change your preferences, and sometimes indulge in a sweet treat. The goal is to give you the knowledge and framework to navigate those situations without feeling like you've "fallen off."

You'll learn the importance of fueling your body with the right balance of macro and micronutrients, rather than simply being told what to eat. Your targets give you direction. Your food choices are yours.

Over time, the goal is to move from "Tell me what to eat" to "I understand how to fuel my body." TRAIN isn't here to control your nutrition. It's here to educate and empower you to take control of it.
03MyFitnessPal — Food Tracking
For these 8 weeks, we track.

Not because I want you counting calories forever, but because data teaches us things that guessing can't.

Your body is an incredibly complex biological system, and the food you eat provides the energy and nutrients it needs to function. Calories (kcal) provide energy, while protein, carbohydrates, fats, and micronutrients each play important roles in how your body functions, performs, and recovers.

Understanding this process gives you something far more valuable than a meal plan: awareness, control, and the ability to make informed choices.

Tracking your food for these 8 weeks allows us to see how much energy you're actually consuming, how you're distributing your macronutrients, and how that intake aligns with your training, recovery, and goals. It turns nutrition from something you guess at into something you can actually understand.

I know tracking can feel tedious at times. That's okay. We're not tracking to obsess — we're tracking to learn.

Use the free version of MyFitnessPal — no premium subscription needed:
  • Go to Goal Settings and set a custom calorie and macro goal. I will provide your specific targets.
  • Enter the numbers from your Nutrition Guide for calories, protein, carbohydrates, and fat.
  • Log your meals as accurately and consistently as you can. Consistency matters more than perfection. Protein is the number I care about most — however, that doesn't make carbs, fats, and micronutrients any less important.
  • In Settings → Sharing & Privacy → Diary Sharing, set your diary to Friends Only and send a request to [coach MyFitnessPal username] so I can review your food log.
You don't have to track your food forever. These 8 weeks are about education, awareness, and learning how your body responds to what you put into it.

The goal isn't to become dependent on tracking. It's to understand your body's energy needs and nutritional requirements well enough that you can eventually make smart decisions without needing an app to tell you what to eat.

Because once you understand the machine, you have far more control over how you fuel it.
04Progress Photos
This is where we capture the part of your progress that numbers can't.

Your weight, measurements, and performance data tell us what's happening in numbers — but your body is three-dimensional, and a photo captures changes that a scale or measurement simply can't. Muscle definition, shape, proportion, posture, and the way your body is changing over time can all tell a deeper story.

I know progress photos can feel uncomfortable at first, but this data is incredibly valuable. The more pieces of the picture we have, the better we can understand your progress. Think of your photos as another measurement — just one that tells a visual story.

Upload once a week when possible, ideally in the same lighting, pose, and clothing (front, side, and back). Consistency makes the changes easier to see over time.

You don't need to love the photo. You just need to take it. Let the data tell the story.

  • Ask questions. If you're unsure about an exercise, your numbers, or why you're doing something, ask. Don't guess — understanding why is part of the process
  • Be honest with your data. You don't need perfect workouts, perfect nutrition, or perfect weeks. You need accurate information so we can make smart decisions from it

Mesocycle 1 — Week 1  ·  Jun 15–21
Foundation
Week 1 isn't about going hard — it's about going smart. This week is about learning the program, dialing in your form, and building a strong foundation. Slow down, be present, and pay attention to what each movement feels like. Ask yourself: What muscles are working? Where should I be feeling this exercise? Don't worry about lifting your heaviest weights yet. Focus on quality over quantity. Master the movement patterns, establish consistency, and trust the process. We're not chasing quick results — we're building something real, something sustainable. Every rep this week is an investment in the strength, confidence, and progress that's coming. Let's get to work.
Mesocycle 1 — Week 2  ·  Jun 22–28
Accumulation
Week 2 is where we start building momentum. By now, you should be feeling the effects of your training — and that's a good thing. Your body is adapting, getting stronger, and preparing for what's next. This week, aim to work toward the top of your prescribed rep ranges. If you reach the upper end of the range and still feel like you have 2 or more reps left in the tank, it's time to increase the weight. Be intentional. Track every set, every rep, and every load. The numbers you record this week will become the foundation for your progress moving forward. Remember: we're not just completing workouts — we're collecting data, building strength, and creating measurable progress. Stay focused, trust the process, and keep showing up. Let's keep stacking wins.
Mesocycle 1 — Week 3  ·  Jun 29–Jul 5
Intensification
Week 3 is where it starts to get real. Over the past two weeks, you've built the foundation. You've learned the movements, established consistency, and gathered the data. Now it's time to turn that foundation into progress. This week, challenge yourself to dig a little deeper. Approach each set with intention and confidence. Trust the strength you've built and don't be afraid to push yourself when the opportunity is there. Your goal isn't to be reckless — it's to be resilient. Stay disciplined with your form, embrace the discomfort that comes with growth, and remember that progress often lives just outside of your comfort zone. Keep tracking your weights, reps, and performance. Small improvements compound quickly, and every workout is another opportunity to become stronger than you were last week. This is what you've been building toward. Let's see what you're capable of.
Week 4 — Performance  ·  Jul 6–12
Performance Week
Take a moment to appreciate what you've accomplished. Four weeks ago, this program was brand new. Today, you're moving with more confidence, more strength, and a deeper understanding of your body. The work you've put in is starting to show. This week is about performance. Not perfection. Not proving anything. Performance. Challenge yourself to match or exceed the numbers you've recorded over the past few weeks. Lean into your strength, trust your preparation, and give yourself permission to see what you're capable of. Progress isn't always measured by the scale or the mirror. Sometimes it's the extra rep, the heavier dumbbell, the improved form, or the confidence that comes from doing hard things consistently. One month down. Countless opportunities ahead. Finish this phase strong — you've earned it.
Week 5 — New Baseline  ·  Jul 13–19
New Baseline
Take a moment to recognize how far you've come. Five weeks ago, you were learning the movements, building confidence, and establishing new habits. Today, you're stronger, more skilled, and more capable than when you started. What once felt challenging is now your baseline. This week marks the beginning of a new phase. You'll see some new exercise variations and progressions designed to keep you moving forward. Remember: we're not starting over — we're building from a stronger foundation. Approach these new movements with the same intention and focus that got you here. Stay patient as you learn, trust the process, and continue tracking your progress. The goal isn't to be perfect, it's to keep moving forward.
Week 6 — Recovery Week  ·  Jul 20–26
Recovery Week
You've spent the last five weeks building strength, accumulating volume, and pushing your body to adapt. But progress isn't just built through hard training — it's built through recovery. This week is your recommended deload, and it's one of the most important weeks of the entire program. By now, fatigue has accumulated. Your muscles, joints, and nervous system have been working hard, and this week gives your body the opportunity to absorb all of that work and come back stronger. Remember: you don't grow during training — you grow from recovering from training. We'll keep the same movements this week, but reduce the demands. Perform just 2 working sets per exercise and decrease your loads to roughly 60% of what you've been using. Move with intention, focus on quality, and enjoy the opportunity to train without having to push. Think of this week as an investment, not a step backward. Recovery isn't a reward you earn — it's part of the process. Trust it. The strength you've been building doesn't disappear here. In fact, some of your biggest gains are waiting on the other side of this week. Recover well. The next phase is coming. 💪
Recovery week
Same movements. 2 sets. 60% of your best loads. No conditioning finishers. Supercompensation happens right here — let it work.
Week 7 — Peak Week  ·  Jul 27–Aug 2
Peak Week
This is your strongest week yet. Over the last six weeks, you've laid the foundation, built momentum, pushed your limits, recovered with intention, and prepared your body for this moment. Peak Week is where all of that work comes together. You should be feeling refreshed, confident, and ready to perform. This week, we're turning the dial up. Push your primary lifts to an RPE of 8–9–10 (meaning your sets should feel challenging, with about 2 reps left in the tank at an RPE 8, 1 rep left at an RPE 9, and no reps left at an RPE 10). Trust the numbers you've built, trust your technique, and don't be afraid to challenge yourself. Remember, intensity doesn't mean sacrificing form. Stay disciplined, move with purpose, and make every rep count. Quality always comes first. This is your opportunity to showcase the strength you've earned. The weight you're lifting today isn't random — it's the result of weeks of consistency, patience, and effort. Be present. Be confident. Be proud of how far you've come. You've earned every rep. Now go get it.
Week 8 — Finish Strong  ·  Aug 3–9
Finish Strong
Eight weeks ago, you made a commitment to yourself — and you honored it. Through every phase of this program, you've shown up. You learned the movements, built momentum, pushed your limits, recovered with intention, and discovered just how strong you're capable of becoming. This week isn't about chasing perfection. It's about finishing with purpose. As you move through your workouts, take time to reflect on how far you've come. Compare your Week 1 numbers to where you are today. Those improvements didn't happen by accident — they were earned through consistency, patience, and showing up week after week. That gap between where you started and where you are today? That's yours. Push hard this week, but don't forget to appreciate the process that got you here. Strength isn't built in a single workout. It's built through the hundreds of reps, the small decisions, and the promises you've kept to yourself along the way. When this week is complete, don't just move on. Pause. Assess. Reflect. Celebrate. What got stronger? What felt better? Where did you surprise yourself? What do you want to accomplish next? Because this isn't the end. It's your new beginning. You've built something real over these last eight weeks. Now let's build the next chapter.

Your estimated maintenance is ~1,943 calories/day — that's roughly what your body burns in a day at your current size and activity level, the number where weight stays the same. To lose fat, you have to eat below that number consistently. That gap between maintenance and what you actually eat is your calorie deficit, and it's the thing that actually drives fat loss — protein, food choice, and timing just determine how good you feel and how much muscle you keep while it happens.
LevelDaily CaloriesDeficitEst. Weekly Change
Maintenance ~1,943 — Weight stays the same
Moderate deficit your plan ~1,700 -243 cal ~0.5 lb/week
Aggressive deficit ~1,443 -500 cal ~1 lb/week
We're starting at the moderate deficit, not the aggressive one. A smaller deficit is easier to hit your protein floor on, easier to recover from your training sessions on, and far easier to actually stick to. A bigger deficit drops the scale faster on paper, but raises the risk of losing muscle along with the fat, and makes crash-and-overeat cycles more likely. If progress stalls a few weeks in — which happens naturally as maintenance drops with body weight — we can step toward the more aggressive range. We don't start there.
These numbers are your daily targets, not a strict meal plan. Your protein target is the floor, not the ceiling. Structure your meals around how you actually eat day to day — the goal is to hit the numbers in a way that fits your schedule, not to force a meal structure that doesn't.

Protein

Repairs and builds the muscle tissue you break down in training — without enough, your body will pull from muscle for energy in a deficit. It's also the most filling macro, which helps with hunger and cravings.

Carbs

Your primary fuel for training output and recovery — they replenish glycogen so your sessions don't feel flat, and they directly support brain function and mood.

Fat

Drives hormone production and supports brain health, and helps you absorb fat-soluble vitamins from your food.
