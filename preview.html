How This Works
01Your Training ProgramIncluded with Self-Guided & 1:1 Coaching
TRAIN is an intelligent training platform built around progressive overload and real performance data. Your program is structured in 8-week blocks, with each week intentionally building on the one before it. TRAIN tells you exactly what to do each session — including exercises, sets, reps, and progression — while tracking the weight and reps you log along the way.

As you TRAIN, your performance data carries forward week to week, so you always know what you did last time and what you're working toward next. Progressive overload is built into the programming, allowing reps, sets, and load to increase strategically throughout the block.

There's no spreadsheets, screenshots, or guessing what to do next. You simply open TRAIN, log your performance, and come back the following week knowing exactly where to pick up. Week 1 establishes your baseline. Week 8 reflects how far you've progressed.

02Your Nutrition GuideIncluded with Self-Guided & 1:1 Coaching
What you eat plays a major role in how you feel, perform, recover, and ultimately see results. But the goal isn't to put you on a meal plan you have to follow forever — it's to empower you with the knowledge and tools to take charge of your own nutrition.

TRAIN gives you personalized calorie, protein, carbohydrate, fat, and fiber targets, along with meal ideas based on foods you actually enjoy. More importantly, it helps you understand why those nutrients matter, how they fuel your body, and how to make smart choices that support your goals.

Because real life isn't a meal plan. You're going to eat at restaurants, travel, have busy days, change your preferences, and sometimes indulge in a sweet treat. The goal is to give you the knowledge and framework to navigate those situations without feeling like you've "fallen off."

You'll learn the importance of fueling your body with the right balance of macro and micronutrients, rather than simply being told what to eat. Your targets give you direction. Your food choices are yours.

Over time, the goal is to move from "Tell me what to eat" to "I understand how to fuel my body." TRAIN isn't here to control your nutrition. It's here to educate and empower you to take control of it.

03MyFitnessPal — Food TrackingIncluded in 1:1 Coaching Only
For these 8 weeks, we track.

Not because I want you counting calories forever, but because data teaches us things that guessing can't.

Your body is an incredibly complex biological system, and the food you eat provides the energy and nutrients it needs to function. Calories (kcal) provide energy, while protein, carbohydrates, fats, and micronutrients each play important roles in how your body functions, performs, and recovers.

Understanding this process gives you something far more valuable than a meal plan: awareness, control, and the ability to make informed choices.

Tracking your food for these 8 weeks allows us to see how much energy you're actually consuming, how you're distributing your macronutrients, and how that intake aligns with your training, recovery, and goals. It turns nutrition from something you guess at into something you can actually understand.

I know tracking can feel tedious at times. That's okay. We're not tracking to obsess — we're tracking to learn.

Use the free version of MyFitnessPal — no premium subscription needed:

  • Log your meals as accurately and consistently as you can. Consistency matters more than perfection. Protein is the number I care about most — however, that doesn't make carbs, fats, and micronutrients any less important.
  • In Settings → Sharing & Privacy → Diary Sharing, set your diary to Friends Only and send a request to Kimvalles7113 so I can review your food log.
You don't have to track your food forever. These 8 weeks are about education, awareness, and learning how your body responds to what you put into it.

The goal isn't to become dependent on tracking. It's to understand your body's energy needs and nutritional requirements well enough that you can eventually make smart decisions without needing an app to tell you what to eat.

Because once you understand the machine, you have far more control over how you fuel it.

This is where we capture the part of your progress that numbers can't.

Your weight, measurements, and performance data tell us what's happening in numbers — but your body is three-dimensional, and a photo captures changes that a scale or measurement simply can't. Muscle definition, shape, proportion, posture, and the way your body is changing over time can all tell a deeper story.

Upload once a week when possible, ideally in the same lighting, pose, and clothing (front, side, and back). Consistency makes the changes easier to see over time.

You don't need to love the photo. You just need to take it. Let the data tell the story.

Good To Know
FAQ
Common questions about training, consistency, and how coaching with TRAIN works.
What if I miss a workout?
Try to complete your programmed number of days in the gym for that week's block. But if you miss a day, just let it go — don't try to squeeze it in or double up to make up for it. Set a goal to get that missed session done the following week instead. Consistency over the full 8 weeks matters more than any single workout.
What if I'm traveling or my week gets disrupted?
Message me ahead of time if you can. I'd rather know what's going on and help you navigate it than have you go silent. We can make a plan that keeps you moving without trying to force a normal week into an abnormal one.
What is progressive overload?
Progressive overload is the process of gradually challenging your body more over time so it has a reason to adapt. That might mean increasing the weight, reps, sets, range of motion, or quality of an exercise as you get stronger.

TRAIN builds progressive overload into your program, so you don't have to guess when or how to progress. Your job is to log what you actually do so you can see your numbers build over time.
How do I know how much weight to use?
Your program will give you a target, but the right weight should challenge you while allowing you to maintain good form and complete your prescribed reps. If you're unsure, start conservatively. Quality reps come before adding weight.
What's the purpose of sets and reps, and why train in a specific rep range?
A rep is one full repetition of an exercise; a set is a group of reps done back-to-back before you rest. Together, they control your total training volume — the total amount of work a muscle does — which is one of the biggest drivers of getting stronger and building muscle.

The rep range you're assigned isn't random — different ranges are better suited to different goals:
Rep RangeBest For
1–5Maximal strength and power
6–12Muscle growth (hypertrophy) — where most of your program lives
13–20+Muscular endurance

Sticking to the prescribed sets and reps matters just as much as the exercise itself — it's what makes your training stimulus consistent from week to week, so we can actually track progress and know when it's time to add weight.
What is RPE or RIR?
RPE (Rate of Perceived Exertion) and RIR (Reps in Reserve) are two ways of describing how hard a set felt, so you and I have a shared language for effort instead of just "it was hard" or "it was easy." RIR tells you how many more reps you could have done before failure. RPE is the same idea on a 1–10 scale, where 10 means you couldn't have done another rep. They're basically mirror images of each other — a set at RPE 8 is the same as a set with 2 reps in reserve.
RPERIRWhat it feels like
≤64+Moderate to light — warm-up range
73Challenging — 3 more reps possible
82Hard — 2 more reps possible
91Very hard — 1 more rep possible
100Max effort — could not do another rep (FAIL)
What is training to failure?
Training to failure means doing reps until your muscles physically can't complete another one with good form — that's RPE 10, 0 RIR. It's a useful tool in the right dose, but it's not something you need every set to make progress. Most of your training will sit a rep or two shy of failure (RPE 7–9) so you can recover, keep your form tight, and stay consistent week to week. If a set calls for failure, I'll flag it specifically.
How does this all fit together — sets, reps, effort, and progressive overload?
Every time you train, you're creating a stimulus — a reason for your body to adapt. Sets and reps determine your volume (how much work you did), RPE/RIR tells you how close to your limit that work was (your intensity), and progressive overload is what turns one hard session into long-term progress: gradually increasing that stimulus over time so your body never runs out of a reason to keep adapting.

Think of it as a loop: you train hard enough to challenge your body, you recover, and you come back slightly stronger or more capable than before. Then you do it again, just a little harder. That loop — stimulus, recovery, adaptation, repeat — is the entire engine behind the program. Logging your numbers each week is what lets us actually see that loop working instead of just hoping it is.
How do I know if I'm training hard enough?
If you have to ask that question, you probably aren't. But in all seriousness — if you finish a workout and feel like you didn't really get a solid session in, send me a video of you performing the exercises. We'll review it together and figure out what's going on, whether it's your effort, your form, or something else entirely.
What should I eat before and after working out?
Timing depends on what you're eating. If it's a full meal — like breakfast or lunch — try to eat it 2–3 hours before your workout so it has time to digest. If you're training first thing in the morning before your first meal and just want something on your stomach, a light snack about 30 minutes beforehand is fine.

I don't recommend training fasted. As your coach, I'd rather you have some fuel in your system before you train — even a small snack — than go in completely empty.

What time of day is best to work out?
Whenever you can consistently schedule it in. Treat your workouts like real appointments — put them on your calendar, and if you miss one, try to reschedule rather than skip it. Some people thrive on 5am sessions, others prefer late morning or a late-night workout. There's no universal "best" time — it's about finding the slot that fits your life and that you'll actually stick to.
What if I can't do an exercise?
Don't just skip it or substitute something randomly. Message me. Whether it's equipment availability, discomfort, mobility, or simply not feeling right for you, I'll help you find an appropriate modification.
What if an exercise hurts?
Pain is different from something simply feeling challenging. If an exercise causes pain, stop and tell me what you're experiencing rather than pushing through it or trying to work around it yourself.
Do I need to be perfect with my workouts and nutrition?
Absolutely not. Missed workouts, imperfect meals, busy weeks, and life happening are all part of being human. What matters is that you stay engaged, communicate, and get back to it.
Can I change my check-in day?
At the beginning of the 8-week block, we'll set a recurring check-in time. I'll try to stay consistent with that, but life happens and things change on both ends — so there's flexibility here. Just let me know if a conflict comes up.
Can I change my photo day?
You can take your progress photos any time during the week — they just need to be uploaded by Sunday. I recommend taking them in the morning, in the same clothes, same spot, and same lighting each time for the most accurate comparison.

Text or email me — whichever is easiest for you.
