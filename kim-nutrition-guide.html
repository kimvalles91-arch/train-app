My estimated maintenance is ~1,900 calories/day — that's roughly what my body burns in a day at my current size and activity level, the number where weight stays the same. To recomp, I need a modest deficit while keeping protein high enough to hold onto muscle. That gap between maintenance and what I actually eat is my calorie deficit — the thing that drives fat loss — while protein, food choice, and timing determine how good I feel and how much muscle I keep while it happens.

Starting at the moderate -250 deficit (~1,650 cal), not the aggressive one. A smaller deficit is easier to hit my 144g protein floor on, easier to recover from training on, and easier to actually stick to. A bigger deficit drops the scale faster on paper but raises the risk of losing muscle along with fat — working against the "gain muscle" half of recomp. If progress stalls a few weeks in, I can step toward the -500 range then. Not starting there.
These numbers are daily targets, not a strict meal plan. 144g protein is the floor, not the ceiling. Structure it around however many meals + shakes actually fit my day — consistency beats a "perfect" structure I won't stick to.

Protein

Repairs and builds the muscle tissue broken down in training — without enough, the body pulls from muscle for energy in a deficit, working against the "gain muscle" half of recomp. Also the most filling macro, which helps manage cravings.

Carbs

Primary fuel for training output and recovery — they replenish glycogen so strength sessions don't feel flat, and they directly support brain function and mood.

Fat

Drives hormone production, supports brain health, and helps absorb fat-soluble vitamins from vegetables. Don't cut it too low.
