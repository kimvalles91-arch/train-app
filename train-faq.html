FAQ
Common questions about training, consistency, and how coaching with TRAIN works.
What if I miss a workout?
Life happens. Pick the plan back up on your next scheduled day rather than trying to "make it up." Consistency over the full 8 weeks matters more than any single session.
What if I'm traveling or my week gets disrupted?
Message me ahead of time if you can. I'd rather know what's going on and help you navigate it than have you go silent. We can make a plan that keeps you moving without trying to force a normal week into an abnormal one.
What is progressive overload?
Progressive overload is the process of gradually challenging your body more over time so it has a reason to adapt. That might mean increasing the weight, reps, sets, range of motion, or quality of an exercise as you get stronger.

TRAIN builds progressive overload into your program, so you don't have to guess when or how to progress. Your job is to log what you actually do so you can see your numbers build over time.
How do I know how much weight to use?
Your program will give you a target, but the right weight should challenge you while allowing you to maintain good form and complete your prescribed reps. If you're unsure, start conservatively. Quality reps come before adding weight.
What is RPE or RIR?
RPE (Rate of Perceived Exertion) and RIR (Reps in Reserve) are two ways of describing how hard a set felt, so you and I have a shared language for effort instead of just "it was hard" or "it was easy." RIR tells you how many more reps you could have done before failure. RPE is the same idea on a 1–10 scale, where 10 means you couldn't have done another rep. They're basically mirror images of each other — a set at RPE 8 is the same as a set with 2 reps in reserve.

What is training to failure?
Training to failure means doing reps until your muscles physically can't complete another one with good form — that's RPE 10, 0 RIR. It's a useful tool in the right dose, but it's not something you need every set to make progress. Most of your training will sit a rep or two shy of failure (RPE 7–9) so you can recover, keep your form tight, and stay consistent week to week. If a set calls for failure, I'll flag it specifically.
What should I eat before and after working out?
A light snack — like a banana or a slice of toast — works well beforehand, and getting some protein in afterward helps with recovery. Timing comes down to how recently you've eaten: if you've had a full meal, give it 2–3 hours before training. If it's just a small snack, 30 minutes is plenty.
What time of day is best to work out?
Whenever you can consistently schedule it in. Treat your workouts like real appointments — put them on your calendar, and if you miss one, try to reschedule rather than skip it. Some people thrive on 5am sessions, others prefer late morning or a late-night workout. There's no universal "best" time — it's about finding the slot that fits your life and that you'll actually stick to.
What if I can't do an exercise?
Don't just skip it or substitute something randomly. Message me. Whether it's equipment availability, discomfort, mobility, or simply not feeling right for you, I'll help you find an appropriate modification.
What if an exercise hurts?
Pain is different from something simply feeling challenging. If an exercise causes pain, stop and tell me what you're experiencing rather than pushing through it or trying to work around it yourself.
Do I need to be perfect with my workouts and nutrition?
Absolutely not. Missed workouts, imperfect meals, busy weeks, and life happening are all part of being human. What matters is that you stay engaged, communicate, and get back to it.
Can I change my check-in day?
At the beginning of the 8-week block, we'll set a recurring check-in time. I'll try to stay consistent with that, but life happens and things change on both ends — so there's flexibility here. Just let me know if a conflict comes up.
Can I change my photo day?
You can take your progress photos any time during the week — they just need to be uploaded by Sunday. I recommend taking them in the morning, in the same clothes, same spot, and same lighting each time for the most accurate comparison.
What's the best way to reach you?
Text or email me — whichever is easiest for you.
