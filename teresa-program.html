Getting Started
How This Works
01Your Training Program
TRAIN is an intelligent training platform built around progressive overload and real performance data. Your program is structured in 8-week blocks, with each week intentionally building on the one before it. TRAIN tells you exactly what to do each session — including exercises, sets, reps, and progression — while tracking the weight and reps you log along the way.

As you TRAIN, your performance data carries forward week to week, so you always know what you did last time and what you're working toward next. Progressive overload is built into the programming, allowing reps, sets, and load to increase strategically throughout the block.

There's no spreadsheets, screenshots, or guessing what to do next. You simply open TRAIN, log your performance, and come back the following week knowing exactly where to pick up. Week 1 establishes your baseline. Week 8 reflects how far you've progressed.

02Your Nutrition Guide
What you eat plays a major role in how you feel, perform, recover, and ultimately see results. But the goal isn't to put you on a meal plan you have to follow forever — it's to empower you with the knowledge and tools to take charge of your own nutrition.

TRAIN gives you personalized calorie, protein, carbohydrate, fat, and fiber targets, along with meal ideas based on foods you actually enjoy. More importantly, it helps you understand why those nutrients matter, how they fuel your body, and how to make smart choices that support your goals.

Because real life isn't a meal plan. You're going to eat at restaurants, travel, have busy days, change your preferences, and sometimes indulge in a sweet treat. The goal is to give you the knowledge and framework to navigate those situations without feeling like you've "fallen off."

You'll learn the importance of fueling your body with the right balance of macro and micronutrients, rather than simply being told what to eat. Your targets give you direction. Your food choices are yours.

Over time, the goal is to move from "Tell me what to eat" to "I understand how to fuel my body." TRAIN isn't here to control your nutrition. It's here to educate and empower you to take control of it.

03MyFitnessPal — Food Tracking
For these 8 weeks, we track.

Not because I want you counting calories forever, but because data teaches us things that guessing can't.

Your body is an incredibly complex biological system, and the food you eat provides the energy and nutrients it needs to function. Calories (kcal) provide energy, while protein, carbohydrates, fats, and micronutrients each play important roles in how your body functions, performs, and recovers.

Understanding this process gives you something far more valuable than a meal plan: awareness, control, and the ability to make informed choices.

Tracking your food for these 8 weeks allows us to see how much energy you're actually consuming, how you're distributing your macronutrients, and how that intake aligns with your training, recovery, and goals. It turns nutrition from something you guess at into something you can actually understand.

I know tracking can feel tedious at times. That's okay. We're not tracking to obsess — we're tracking to learn.

Use the free version of MyFitnessPal — no premium subscription needed:
  • Go to Goal Settings and set a custom calorie and macro goal. I will provide your specific targets.
  • Enter the numbers from your Nutrition Guide for calories, protein, carbohydrates, and fat.
  • Log your meals as accurately and consistently as you can. Consistency matters more than perfection. Protein is the number I care about most — however, that doesn't make carbs, fats, and micronutrients any less important.
  • In Settings → Sharing & Privacy → Diary Sharing, set your diary to Friends Only and send a request to Kimvalles7113 so I can review your food log.
You don't have to track your food forever. These 8 weeks are about education, awareness, and learning how your body responds to what you put into it.

The goal isn't to become dependent on tracking. It's to understand your body's energy needs and nutritional requirements well enough that you can eventually make smart decisions without needing an app to tell you what to eat.

Because once you understand the machine, you have far more control over how you fuel it.

04Progress Photos
This is where we capture the part of your progress that numbers can't.

Your weight, measurements, and performance data tell us what's happening in numbers — but your body is three-dimensional, and a photo captures changes that a scale or measurement simply can't. Muscle definition, shape, proportion, posture, and the way your body is changing over time can all tell a deeper story.

I know progress photos can feel uncomfortable at first, but this data is incredibly valuable. The more pieces of the picture we have, the better we can understand your progress. Think of your photos as another measurement — just one that tells a visual story.

Upload once a week when possible, ideally in the same lighting, pose, and clothing (front, side, and back). Consistency makes the changes easier to see over time.

You don't need to love the photo. You just need to take it. Let the data tell the story.

The Partnership
What To Expect

Coaching works best as a two-way street. Here's what you can count on from me, and what I'll be counting on from you.

From Me

TRAIN handles the structure, progression, and tracking. I'm here to coach you through the process.

  • I review your weekly check-in, progress photos, training data, and nutrition tracking to see the bigger picture
  • I use that information to help guide you throughout your 8-week block and make adjustments when they're actually needed
  • I'm available for questions, exercise substitutions, and form checks between sessions — I'll respond the same day if you reach out before 8pm. Messages after 8pm will get a response the next day.
  • If something isn't working, we'll address it. That might mean adjusting your training, nutrition targets, or approach based on what your data is telling us
  • You'll get honest, direct feedback from me — not just what you want to hear, but what you need to hear to keep moving forward
  • My goal isn't to make you dependent on me. It's to teach you enough about your training and nutrition that you become more confident, capable, and in control of your own progress

From You

Your job is to show up, collect the data, and communicate.

  • Log every workout — weight, reps, and how the session felt. This gives us real performance data to work from
  • Upload your weekly progress photos by Sunday — 4 total: front, back, and both sides. Keep your lighting, clothing, and poses as consistent as possible so we can accurately track visual changes over time
  • Track your food consistently in MyFitnessPal — protein is the priority, but the goal is to build an accurate picture of your overall nutrition
  • Tell me when something is off. Injury, pain, poor sleep, stress, travel, or anything else affecting your training matters. Communicating early gives us the opportunity to adjust before it becomes a missed week
  • Ask questions. If you're unsure about an exercise, your numbers, or why you're doing something, ask. Don't guess — understanding why is part of the process
  • Be honest with your data. You don't need perfect workouts, perfect nutrition, or perfect weeks. You need accurate information so we can make smart decisions from it

Block 2 — Week 9
Re-Entry
This is a straight continuation, not a comeback — Block 2 starts right where Block 1 left off, with no time off in between. Here's where you left off: 135lbs on RDLs, 165lbs on hip thrusts, 150lbs on lat pulldown, 85lbs on barbell rows. Those numbers are your floor, not a ceiling to chase on day one. Week 9 is still a quick calibration week — not because you've been away, but because we're deliberately testing whether your body wants to jump straight back to Block 1's peak numbers or settle in for a session or two first. Move through your working sets at a moderate RPE, pay attention to how things feel, and let the data tell you which it is. Show up, move well, and gather real numbers.
Block 2 — Week 10
Reload
Now that you've got a real read on where you're at, this week is about pushing back toward your Block 1 numbers with intention. Work toward the top of your prescribed rep ranges again — if you hit the top of the range with 2 or more reps left in the tank, add weight next session. Your body adapts fast when it's rebuilding on top of existing strength rather than starting from zero, so don't be surprised if this feels quicker than Block 1's early weeks did. Keep logging everything; these numbers set up the comparison for where you're headed.
Block 2 — Week 11
Reclaim
This is the week you meet or pass your Block 1 peak numbers. A 4th set returns on your main lifts — more total volume, same rep target. If your working weight from Block 1 (135 on RDLs, 150 on lat pulldown, 85 on rows) feels manageable for a full 4 sets at the prescribed reps, that's the green light to call this reclaimed ground and start pushing past it. Trust the strength that's still there — it didn't go anywhere, it just needed reactivating.
Block 2 — Week 12
Surplus
New ground. Everything you lift well this week is genuinely past where Block 1 left off — not a rebuild, a gain. Reps tighten slightly on the heavy compounds so you can push the weight, RPE climbs toward 8-9. This is the payoff week for the calibration and reload weeks — you didn't rush back to your old numbers, so pushing past them now should feel earned rather than forced.
Block 2 — Week 13
New Wave
Sets reset to 3, but your working weight should sit above where Week 9 started — this is a fresh wave built on a higher floor, not a step back. Use this week to reset your rep counts at a slightly lower RPE before the next build-up begins. Lateral movements and any accessory work that's felt easy are fair game to bump up a notch here too.
Block 2 — Week 14 — Recommended Deload
Recovery Week
Same story as Block 1's deload: fatigue has accumulated from four straight weeks of building on top of an already-strong base, and this week lets your body absorb that work. Same movements, 2 working sets, roughly 60% of what you've been using in Week 13. No conditioning finishers. This is where the adaptation from Weeks 9-13 actually consolidates — trust it the same way you did last time.
Recovery week
Same movements. 2 sets. 60% of your best loads. No conditioning finishers. Let the last five weeks of work settle in.
Block 2 — Week 15
Ascend
Coming out of the deload, expect this week to feel noticeably lighter than Week 13 did — that's the recovery working, not lost strength. Build back into your working weights over this session and the next; most people find they're actually a little stronger post-deload than they were going into it. Focus on clean reps as the weight climbs back up.
Block 2 — Week 16
Threshold
A 4th set returns and RPE climbs toward 8 again — this is the volume push that sets up Peak Week. You should be approaching or passing your Week 12 surplus numbers by the end of this week. Stay disciplined with rest periods; the shorter rest at higher volume is what makes this week demanding.
Block 2 — Week 17 — Peak Week
Peak Week
Heaviest loads of Block 2, right here. Push your primary lifts to an RPE of 8-9 (2 reps left in the tank at an 8, 1 rep left at a 9) with rest stretching to 90-120 seconds so you can actually recover between sets. Everything you've built since Week 9 — the calibration, the reload, the new wave, the recovery — was preparing your body for exactly this week. Trust your technique under the heavier loads and let the numbers reflect the work.
Block 2 — Week 18 — Finish Strong
Consolidate
Ten weeks ago you came back from a full block off and had to find out where you actually stood. Compare that Week 9 number to what you're lifting today — that gap is entirely yours, and it's bigger than the gap you built in Block 1, because this time you were rebuilding on top of real strength instead of starting from scratch. This week isn't about a new max, it's about locking in confident, controlled numbers you can carry into Block 3. When it's done: pause, compare Week 9 to Week 18 side by side, and start thinking about what Day 3's new movements should look like — that conversation starts now.

Your estimated maintenance is ~1,900 calories/day — that's roughly what your body burns in a day at your current size and activity level, the number where weight stays the same. To lose fat, you have to eat below that number consistently. That gap between maintenance and what you actually eat is your calorie deficit, and it's the thing that actually drives fat loss — protein, food choice, and timing just determine how good you feel and how much muscle you keep while it happens.
LevelDaily CaloriesDeficitEst. Weekly Change

Moderate deficit your plan ~1,650 -250 cal ~0.5 lb/week

We're starting you at the moderate -250 deficit (~1,650 cal), not the aggressive one. A smaller deficit is easier to hit your 130g protein floor on, easier to recover from your lifting sessions on, and far easier to actually stick to — which matters given your schedule and the sweet-treat pattern you flagged. A bigger deficit drops the scale faster on paper, but raises the risk of losing muscle along with the fat (working against the "gain muscle" half of recomp) and makes crash-and-overeat cycles more likely. If progress stalls a few weeks in — which happens naturally as your maintenance drops with your weight — we can step toward the -500 range. We don't start there.
These numbers are your daily targets, not a strict meal plan. 130g protein is non-negotiable: it's the floor, not the ceiling. You eat 1–2 meals + a shake, so we're structuring it as one ~50g protein shake + two meals at 34–38g protein each, plus a snack to round things out — that combination lands you at 130g protein without forcing a 5-meals-a-day structure you don't have time for between clients.

Protein

Repairs and builds the muscle tissue you break down in training — without enough, your body will pull from muscle for energy in a deficit, which works against the "gain muscle" half of recomp. It's also the most filling macro, which helps with the snacking/munchies pattern.

Carbs

Your primary fuel for training output and recovery — they replenish glycogen so your strength sessions don't feel flat, and they directly support brain function and mood (this matters with your schedule and fatigue).

Fat

Drives hormone production — relevant given the hormonal piece you flagged with your IUD. Also supports brain health and helps you absorb fat-soluble vitamins from your veggies.

Standard Protein Shake

~430 kcal · 50P / 35C / 10F
  • 2 scoops plant protein powder
  • 1 cup unsweetened almond or oat milk (check label for lactose-free)
  • 1 large banana or 1 cup berries
  • 1 tsp peanut or almond butter
Swap: water instead of milk for fewer calories if you're running tight that day · add a full tbsp of nut butter instead of a tsp on training days for extra fuel

Greek Yogurt + Egg White Bowl

~430 kcal · 50P / 35C / 9F
  • 1 1/4 cups Greek yogurt (lactose-light — usually fine, test your tolerance)
  • 1 cup carton egg whites, scrambled on the side
  • 1/2 cup oats or a sliced banana
Swap: cottage cheese instead of yogurt — also low-lactose · use this version on days you want savory instead of a shake
Lactose note: both Greek yogurt and cottage cheese are naturally lower-lactose than milk. If either bothers your IBS, swap back to the plant-based shake.

Tofu or Edamame Rice Bowl

~560 kcal · 38P / 65C / 16F

  • Roasted broccoli, zucchini, or cauliflower

Swap: quinoa or a tortilla wrap for the rice · any liked fibrous veg works here

Shrimp or Salmon Bowl
