Your estimated maintenance is ~1,900 calories/day — that's roughly what your body burns in a day at your current size and activity level, the number where weight stays the same. To lose fat, you have to eat below that number consistently. That gap between maintenance and what you actually eat is your calorie deficit, and it's the thing that actually drives fat loss — protein, food choice, and timing just determine how good you feel and how much muscle you keep while it happens.

We're starting you at the moderate -250 deficit (~1,650 cal), not the aggressive one. A smaller deficit is easier to hit your 110g protein floor on, easier to recover from your lifting sessions on, and far easier to actually stick to — which matters given your schedule and the sweet-treat pattern you flagged. A bigger deficit drops the scale faster on paper, but raises the risk of losing muscle along with the fat (working against the "gain muscle" half of recomp) and makes crash-and-overeat cycles more likely. If progress stalls a few weeks in — which happens naturally as your maintenance drops with your weight — we can step toward the -500 range. We don't start there.
These numbers are your daily targets, not a strict meal plan. 110g protein is non-negotiable: it's the floor, not the ceiling. You eat 1–2 meals + a shake, so we're structuring it as one ~55g protein shake + two meals at 25–30g protein each — that gets you to 100g+ without forcing a 5-meals-a-day structure you don't have time for between clients.

Protein

Repairs and builds the muscle tissue you break down in training — without enough, your body will pull from muscle for energy in a deficit, which works against the "gain muscle" half of recomp. It's also the most filling macro, which helps with the snacking/munchies pattern.

Carbs

Your primary fuel for training output and recovery — they replenish glycogen so your strength sessions don't feel flat, and they directly support brain function and mood (this matters with your schedule and fatigue).

Fat

Drives hormone production — relevant given the hormonal piece you flagged with your IUD. Also supports brain health and helps you absorb fat-soluble vitamins from your veggies.

Standard Protein Shake

~380 kcal · 55P / 25C / 10F
  • 1.5–2 scoops plant protein powder
  • 1 cup unsweetened almond or oat milk (check label for lactose-free)
  • 1 banana or ½ cup berries
  • 1 tbsp peanut or almond butter
Swap: water instead of milk for fewer calories if you're running tight that day · add chia/flax for extra fat + fiber

Greek Yogurt + Egg White Bowl

~400 kcal · 50P / 30C / 10F
  • 1 cup Greek yogurt (lactose-light — usually fine, test your tolerance)
  • 3/4 cup carton egg whites, scrambled on the side
  • 1/2 cup oats or a sliced banana
Swap: cottage cheese instead of yogurt — also low-lactose · use this version on days you want savory instead of a shake
Lactose note: both Greek yogurt and cottage cheese are naturally lower-lactose than milk. If either bothers your IBS, swap back to the plant-based shake.

Tofu or Edamame Rice Bowl

~520 kcal · 28P / 55C / 18F
  • 5–6oz baked or pan-seared tofu, or 1.5 cups edamame
  • 3/4 cup cooked white or brown rice
  • Roasted broccoli, zucchini, or cauliflower
  • Drizzle of olive oil + soy sauce
Swap: quinoa or a tortilla wrap for the rice · any liked fibrous veg works here

Shrimp or Salmon Bowl

~510 kcal · 30P / 50C / 16F
  • 5oz shrimp (pre-cooked frozen shrimp works great for low-effort cooking) or 4-5oz salmon
  • Sweet potato or 3/4 cup rice
  • Asparagus, green beans, or salad greens
  • Avocado or olive oil for fat
Swap: frozen pre-cooked shrimp removes the "iffy about prepping seafood" issue entirely — just reheat

Lentil or Chickpea Bowl

~480 kcal · 26P / 55C / 14F
  • 1 cup cooked lentils or chickpeas
  • 1/2 cup quinoa or couscous
  • Mixed roasted veg (carrots, brussels sprouts, tomatoes)
  • Tahini or olive oil drizzle
Swap: black beans for lentils · this one travels well for between-clients eating

Tempeh or Egg White Stir-fry

~470 kcal · 28P / 45C / 16F
  • 5oz tempeh, cubed and seared, or 1 cup egg whites + 1/2 cup chickpeas for protein
  • Whole wheat pasta or rice noodles
  • Mushrooms, peppers, cucumber, or any liked fibrous veg
  • Sesame oil + a little peanut butter for sauce
Swap: corn or a bagel-based side instead of pasta if you want variety

Protein-Forward (beats the munchies)

~180-220 kcal · 15-20P
  • Cottage cheese + berries
  • Protein bar (check sugar alcohols if IBS-sensitive)
  • Edamame, lightly salted
  • Hard-boiled egg whites + a piece of fruit
The "stoner munchies/sweet treats" pattern is easier to manage when a protein-forward snack is sitting in the fridge ready to go — decision fatigue is the enemy, not willpower.

Quick & Easy

  • Apple + peanut or almond butter

  • Sparkling water + a protein bar if you need something sweet-feeling
